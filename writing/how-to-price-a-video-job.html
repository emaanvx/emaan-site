How I’d price the job you’re about to undercharge

Your quote is low because you priced the only part of the job you can see.

Somebody asks what you charge for a day. You answer with a day rate. In that one sentence you have agreed to be paid for the hours, which is the part a less experienced operator could also supply, and you have given away the part that is actually yours, which is deciding what gets shot and in what order. Jonathan Stark puts it in four words on his site: sell your brains, not your hands.

Three questions before the number

What is the deliverable, counted? Not a day of shooting. Three vertical cuts, two rounds of revisions, delivered on a named day. A day is an input. A cut is the thing they are buying, and a client can picture a cut, which means they can agree to it.

Who carries it if the brief changes? If you reshoot for free when they change their mind, you are selling insurance and charging nothing for it. Settle how many rounds are in and what the next one costs before anybody says yes.

Where does it run, and for how long? The same footage is not the same product in a one-off post and in a paid ad running all quarter. That is not me being clever with it. Adobe Stock sorts its licences by what you do with the file rather than by the file, so the stock industry settled this years before either of us turned up.

Then the number

The three answers give you a scope, and the number comes off the scope. There is one test for it: a yes should not make you wince. If they agree straight away and you feel a drop, you did not price the job. You priced your nerve.

It is also the number you can say out loud without a preamble, which matters more than it sounds like it should. Most quotes get discounted in the sending rather than in the negotiation. You shave it because you cannot explain it, and a client who can hear that the number is soft is hearing something real.

None of this makes the number bigger on its own. It makes the number explainable, and the argument stops being the one you have with yourself.

If you want to run this on a quote you are actually about to send, that is what the mentorship is for.
