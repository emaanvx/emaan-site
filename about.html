I decide what it says,
and what it refuses to say.

I'm Emaan Khan, an independent creative director working out of Toronto. My job is judgement: what a brand says, how it looks saying it, what it turns down, and what the rule is when I'm not there to make the call.

The narrowness is deliberate

I work on short-form video. Not brand identity, not campaigns in general, not whatever the brief happens to be that quarter. Short-form video, and the thinking that has to happen before a frame of it exists.

That is an unusual thing to advertise, because the obvious commercial move is to be available for more. I've found the opposite is true. Nobody remembers a generalist. The work that's worth doing comes from people who already know exactly what they need and are looking for the person who does that one thing properly.

So: one format, studied to the point of boredom. I know what the first three seconds are doing, why a cut lands where it lands, and which of the hundred things a brand wants to say is the one that survives contact with a feed. That is a small body of knowledge. It is also, in this format, nearly all of it.

How I work

I came up operating. I shot, I cut, I wrote the hooks, and I still do — not out of nostalgia, but because direction given by someone who can't execute is just opinion with a title in front of it. I can tell you where the cut goes because I've made that cut several hundred times and been wrong about it plenty.

I've directed teams of editors, designers and videographers, including for six months while living in the Netherlands with the team spread across thirteen countries. That taught me more about direction than any year in one room did. When nobody can walk over to your desk, every vague instruction you have ever given comes back as a wrong deliverable. You learn to be precise, or you learn to redo everything twice.

What I actually believe

Most brands don't have a content problem. They have a taste problem, and they've been sold volume as the cure. More posts will not fix a confused one. The decision is upstream of the camera, and almost nobody wants to make it, because making it means saying no to four other ideas that were also fine.

And: a finished video with no thinking attached is an advertisement, not a body of work. That's why the writing here is about the build rather than the result.

Why I mentor so few people

There's a whole economy now selling scale to people who need attention. Thousand-person communities, course libraries, a Discord nobody reads. I have no interest in it.

I take on a handful of people at a time, because the only version of mentorship that works is the one where I know your last three pieces without looking them up. Everyone else is welcome to read everything I publish here, free, for as long as they like. That's the community. The mentorship is the small room inside it.
